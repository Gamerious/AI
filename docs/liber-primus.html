All nine solved sections, and why the other nine resist

The Liber Primus is 15,933 runes long, and 2,977 of them have been read. This is a reproduction of every one of those from the raw transcription, and a measurement of what is left — which carries exactly one statistical fingerprint, and that fingerprint rules out most of the classical toolbox.

A rule that runs backwards badly

Why a ciphertext ᚠ means nothing on its own

The community notes on the polyalphabetic pages say “clear text F is not encrypted.” That is a rule about the plaintext: a plaintext ᚠ is emitted unchanged and does not consume a key character. Re-encrypting the known plaintext under that rule reproduces the ciphertext exactly; without it, only 28%.

Run backwards it is ambiguous, because ᚠ has gematria index 0 — so a ᚠ in the ciphertext is either an untouched plaintext F, or an ordinary rune that happened to encrypt onto zero. Both happen, and the collisions outnumber the real ones.

Each one forks the decryption. The toolkit resolves them by search rather than by eye: states are keyed by key-position and n-gram context, ranked by an English model, and the surviving path is the plaintext. That model has to live in rune space, not latin — the Gematria Primus folds TH, EO, NG, OE, IA, EA and AE into single symbols, so THINGS is three runes, not six, and scoring after transliteration measures the wrong alphabet.

Two things surfaced from the diffs. The WISDOM headline genuinely encrypts to WIDSOM — the ciphertext is ᛒᛗᚱᚦᚠᛈ, where WISDOM would need ᛒᛗᚫᛁᚠᛈ. And the reference translation drops an O from FOLLOWNG. The book's first page warns against editing it, “either the words or their numbers.”

The measurement

The open pages avoid repeating themselves

Index of coincidence 1.000. No differential spike at any lag from 1 to 200. No peak in a key-length scan from 1 to 60. The off-diagonal bigram table is flat to within noise (χ² 841.7 on 811 degrees of freedom, z = +0.76). By every standard handle the text is random — except for one thing.

  • adjacency onlyRepeats at gap 2, 3, 4 and 5 all sit at chance, 3.45%. Only immediate neighbours are suppressed.
  • not a resetIt holds within words and across word, line and page boundaries alike, so it is not a per-word or per-line key reset.
  • not a runeThe deficit is uniform over all 29 runes. No single symbol carries it.
  • not a scribeIt reproduces on a second, independent transcription — 13,136 runes, 89 observed against 452.9 expected, z = −17.40. It belongs to the book.

Discrimination

What could produce that

So the open pages behave like a stream cipher whose key is indistinguishable from random, plus a partial constraint against repeating a rune immediately. The obvious candidate for that constraint — a cumulative cipher, where c[i] = c[i-1] + p[i] and a repeat means a plaintext F — is ruled out twice over: it predicts repeats at the plaintext F rate of 1.63%, not the observed 0.66%, and it leaves heavy structure in the bigram table. Inverting it recovers noise.

Negative result

What was searched, and found nothing

The battery is validated on positive controls before it is trusted: it rediscovers atbash on 0.0, atbash+3 on 0.3, φ(pₙ) on 0.16, DIVINITY on 0.1 and FIRFUMFERENFE on 0.5, each by 0.6 to 1.5 nats over the runner-up.

The integer streams include the primes, φ(pₙ), φ(n), Fibonacci, Lucas, triangular numbers, squares, prime gaps, prime partial sums, multiples of 3301 and the digits of π and e. None of this proves the pages are unbreakable. It does mean the answer is not a keyword, not a named integer sequence, not a transposition, and not any periodic key — which is most of what a classical toolbox holds, and is consistent with a decade of community failure.

The earlier hunts

2012 and 2013

Both were solved the year they ran. They were chains of steganography, hidden services, a phone number and a book code rather than cipher work, so most steps cannot be recomputed from what survives. Two can.

  • 2012 The archived Mabinogion file is The Lady of the Fountain under a Vigenère with the 23-letter key KCOHTGSMHIRATHOSOTNABCA, reset at every line. The reset is what hides it: read as one stream the key looks aperiodic. Recovered from the cipher/plaintext pair; the round trip is exact over all 9,525 letters.
  • 2013 Four 256-byte blobs from the hidden services, one derived from Liber Primus pages 49–51. They are not RSA moduli — three of the four are even, pairwise GCDs are trivial, and trial division to 10⁵ leaves 2013-bit remainders. Byte entropy of 7.12–7.28 bits is exactly what 256 random bytes give. Signature or ciphertext material, nothing factorable.
